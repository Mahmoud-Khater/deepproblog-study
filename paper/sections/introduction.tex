\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Breast cancer is among the most commonly diagnosed cancers worldwide and a major cause of cancer-related mortality in women~\cite{sung2021global}. Mammography and breast ultrasound are central to detection and diagnostic assessment. Machine learning and deep learning have achieved strong performance on medical image analysis tasks, including breast imaging classification and computer-aided diagnosis~\cite{litjens2017survey,balkenende2022application}.

Despite these advances, purely neural models can be difficult to interpret because the final prediction need not expose an explicit reasoning process understandable to medical professionals. In high-stakes clinical settings, explainability and trust are especially important~\cite{rudin2019stop,vandervelden2022xai}. One approach is to introduce an intermediate representation of clinically meaningful visual concepts and combine those concepts with explicit domain knowledge~\cite{koh2020concept}.

Neuro-symbolic AI provides a framework for combining neural perception with symbolic reasoning~\cite{garcez2002neural,garcez2023neurosymbolic}. DeepProbLog is particularly relevant because it integrates neural predicates with probabilistic logic programming, enabling reasoning under uncertainty and, depending on the formulation, differentiable learning~\cite{manhaeve2018deepproblog,manhaeve2021neural}.

This paper investigates a neuro-symbolic breast cancer diagnosis pipeline in which a neural model extracts visual concepts and DeepProbLog performs probabilistic logical reasoning over those concepts. Medical knowledge inspired by BI-RADS~\cite{acr2013birads} will be investigated as the symbolic component. BI-RADS is treated as a source of standardized terminology and assessment criteria, not as author-invented diagnostic rules. The objective is to evaluate whether concept-level perception plus probabilistic symbolic reasoning can provide clinically meaningful intermediate representations, support interpretable reasoning traces, and enable comparison against conventional neural baselines. Candidate datasets include BUSI~\cite{aldhabyani2020busi} and CBIS-DDSM~\cite{lee2017cbis}.

The contributions of this study are: (i)~a neuro-symbolic architecture combining neural visual concept extraction with probabilistic logical reasoning; (ii)~a representation of uncertain neural predictions as probabilistic facts; (iii)~integration of verified domain knowledge into diagnostic reasoning; (iv)~a planned comparison against neural baselines; and (v)~an analysis framework for reasoning-trace interpretability. These describe the proposed investigation; improved accuracy remains a hypothesis to be tested.
